Vertical Dreams

A concept brand and website for Vertical Dreams, an independent climbing gym in Manchester, New Hampshire. Its logo and website hadn't changed in years, so I set myself a challenge: how far could I bring an independent gym up to today's standards? The answer was a new logo and brand system, a review of the old site, and a new homepage.

Client

Self-initiated concept

Year

2026

What I did

Logo, brand system, website review, homepage concept

The context

Climbing has outgrown the garage

Indoor climbing and bouldering have boomed. What used to be a niche, dusty hobby, a homemade training board at the back of someone's workshop, is now big business. In October 2026, five European bouldering brands came together as the International Bouldering Alliance: 39 gyms across four countries, with plans to grow by buying more. Large groups like this have raised the bar on how a gym looks, how easy it is to book, and how welcoming it is to people who've never climbed.

Small independents like Vertical Dreams can get left behind, and the brand is usually where it shows first. Their logo and website looked like they hadn't been touched since the early 2000s. I wanted to see how far I could bring an independent gym up to today's standards.

Most people coming through the door now are beginners, so everything had to feel welcoming first, without losing the energy that makes climbing fun.

Brand

The logo

The mark is a climber caught mid-move, with their arms and legs arranged to spell V and D. It sits inside an angular, shard-like shape, like a hold or a slab of rock. It works in blue, red, black or reversed out of a colour.

Brand

Colours off the walls

I took the palette straight from the climbing walls: VD Blue, a softer blue, a red kept for the main buttons, plus a warm white and a soft black. Climbing is for everyone, so I checked the palette against the three most common types of colour blindness before signing it off.

Type is Manrope throughout. Its heaviest weight for headings, regular for everything else.

Website

The old website

The site was a WordPress build from around 2015. I wrote a full review before designing anything. Three things stood out:

  • Eight top-level menus, some with seven items, and duplicates.
  • After the move from Nashua to Manchester, the site still offered both locations, so it wasn't clear where the gym actually was.
  • Missing alt text, copyright still 2017, and a dead 2015 Meetup link.

Website

The new homepage

A big full-width photo to open, then straight into the question every new visitor has: which session is for me? New and social, adults, or juniors. Next comes a block just for first-timers, then the community, the different ways to climb, simple pricing, a few words from members and a short FAQ.

I kept red for the four buttons that matter most, like booking a first session. Everything else is blue, black and white. Photos and cards are cut into angular shapes that echo the logo, with a slightly hand-drawn outline.
