IT4IT™
Explainer Videos

A series of seven explainer animations for The Open Group's IT4IT standard. I didn't animate them. I led the project and turned a very technical specification into something a studio could animate and a busy IT leader could follow.

Client

The Open Group

Year

2024

My role

Project lead, script and storyboards

Format

7 explainer videos

One of the seven films in the series

The problem

The hard part came before any animation

IT4IT is a reference architecture for running the business of IT. It's important, and it's also dense. The spec talks in value streams like Strategy to Portfolio and Detect to Correct, functional components and data objects. Nobody watches a short video to learn that vocabulary. They watch it to find out why it matters to them.

So my job was to find the story inside the spec and leave the jargon behind.

  1. The spec

    A long, technical standard written by experts, for experts.

  2. The script

    Agreed with the IT4IT Forum, section by section.

  3. Storyboards

    Seven of them, drawn by me before any animation.

  4. Studio drafts

    Feedback from the Forum, sent back as one clear list each round.

  5. Seven films

    Published on YouTube and across the Forum's pages.

Working with the IT4IT Forum

From standard to script

I worked closely with the IT4IT Forum, the group of members who write and maintain the standard. We went through it section by section and agreed what each video needed to say, who it was for and what could be left out. That became the script.

From the script I drew the storyboards myself, all seven of them, so everyone could see how each idea would play out on screen before we spent money on animation.

Working with the studio

One voice for the studio

Once the Forum signed off the storyboards, I briefed the animation studio and became their single point of contact. They sent drafts. I gathered feedback from the Forum, untangled the comments that disagreed with each other, and sent back one clear set of changes each round.

That last part matters more than it sounds. A room full of experts will always find something to correct. Keeping the studio focused, and the videos simple, was most of the job.

The finished series went out on YouTube and features on the IT4IT Forum's blogs and pages.
