<section class="proj-section reveal">

  <div class="container">

    <p class="sec-label">Overview</p>

    <div class="overview-grid">

      <div>

        <p class="overview-lead">A brand update, not a redesign.</p>

        <p class="overview-body">The Open Group is a 30-year-old standards consortium trusted by 900+ member organisations and 100,000+ certified enterprise architects worldwide. That institutional credibility is the brand's greatest asset — but the visual identity had accumulated inconsistencies that quietly undermined it.</p>

        <p class="overview-body">The logo lockup drifted off its own grid. Sub-brands multiplied without a template. The colour palette had eight swatches and no defined roles. Typography defaulted to a single voice. Stock photography stood in for genuine visual thinking. And the print brand manual had no connection to the CSS already running on the live website.</p>

        <p class="overview-body">The task was precise: bring the visual identity into the modern era without losing what makes it recognisable. The O symbol, the name, and the brand equity in TOGAF, ArchiMate, FACE, OSDU, and UNIX were non-negotiable anchors. Six targeted calibrations — not a redesign, but strategic refinements — delivered a scalable system any team could maintain and extend.</p>

      </div>

      <div>

      </div>

    </div>

  </div>

</section>


<section class="proj-section reveal">

  <div class="container">

    <p class="sec-label">Six Calibrations</p>

    <h2 class="sec-head">Strategic refinements, not redesign</h2>

    <p class="sec-body">Each calibration fixed a specific weakness without replacing the asset that carried it.</p>


    <div class="calib-grid">

      <div class="calib-card">

        <div class="calib-num">01</div>

        <h3 class="calib-title">Logo Lockup</h3>

        <p class="calib-desc">The composition, proportions and colours were preserved. The fix was purely typographic: aligning THE / GROUP to an eight-unit baseline grid, dropping the O onto the same baseline, and eliminating the floating void that made the mark feel unresolved.</p>

      </div>

      <div class="calib-card">

        <div class="calib-num">02</div>

        <h3 class="calib-title">Sub-brand Template</h3>

        <p class="calib-desc">Rather than commissioning bespoke marks for every forum and workgroup, a single endorsement template was designed — one O symbol, one name, one strap — colour-coded by entity type. Any new sub-brand can be generated in minutes.</p>

      </div>

      <div class="calib-card">

        <div class="calib-num">03</div>

        <h3 class="calib-title">Colour with Roles</h3>

        <p class="calib-desc">The existing eight swatches were assigned functional roles: Primary (Logo Blue) for authority and navigation, Accent (Logo Green) for calls to action, with Neutral, Secondary, Surface, and semantic tokens for Success, Warning, and Error states.</p>

      </div>

      <div class="calib-card">

        <div class="calib-num">04</div>

        <h3 class="calib-title">Type Pairing</h3>

        <p class="calib-desc">The Open Group already owned Times New Roman (wordmark) and Arial (body copy). Extending Times into display headlines created a tonal range — authoritative for standards titles, editorial for pull quotes — without a single new font license.</p>

      </div>

      <div class="calib-card">

        <div class="calib-num">05</div>

        <h3 class="calib-title">Diagram Kit</h3>

        <p class="calib-desc">An audience of enterprise architects reads diagrams for a living. A kit of five brand-true components — Band, Block, Arrow, Dot, Label — means any author can produce on-brand visual IP in under a minute, replacing generic stock photography.</p>

      </div>

      <div class="calib-card">

        <div class="calib-num">06</div>

        <h3 class="calib-title">Design System Tokens</h3>

        <p class="calib-desc">The brand manual ran from print rules. The live website already used a CSS token file. Merging the two added typography, spacing, and component patterns — making the design system the single source of truth: importable, versioned, and directly usable by developers.</p>

      </div>

    </div>


    <img src="3-colours.png" alt="Colour palette with roles" class="visual-full">

  </div>

</section>

    <h2 class="sec-head">Applied across every surface</h2>


    <div class="app-section">

      <div class="app-text">

        <h3>A scalable, maintainable system</h3>

        <p>The system was applied across every surface in scope: redesigned homepage prototype, LinkedIn templates (company page, post, carousel, profile banner), conference signage and name badges for Houston 2026, business cards, letterhead, and HTML email signature.</p>

        <p>Six small changes produced a coherent, scalable system — one that any designer or developer in the organisation can use without referring back to a creative team for every decision.</p>

<section class="proj-section reveal">

  <div class="container">

    <p class="sec-label">Result</p>

    <h2 class="sec-head">A brand that scales</h2>

    <p class="sec-body">The homepage redesign demonstrated the type pairing, token system, and dual-level navigation working together as a live prototype. The conference collateral showed the same lockup, palette, and type pair carrying across formats from a 2-metre pull-up banner to an 85mm business card. The sub-brand template removed the need for bespoke design work on every new forum or consortium.</p>

    <p class="sec-body" style="margin-top:16px">By closing the gap between the print brand manual and the live CSS, the refresh created something the previous identity lacked: <em style="color:var(--text)">a brand that can be maintained at scale, by a team, over time — without drifting.</em></p>
